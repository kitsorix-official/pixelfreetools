Legal & Marketplace Guide

Can You Sell Pixel Art?

Yes - it's your copyright from the moment you draw it. Here's where to sell, how licensing works, and what to avoid.

The Good News

Your pixel art is copyrighted the moment you make it

In almost every country, original artwork is protected by copyright automatically as soon as you create it and fix it in a tangible form - you don't need to register it or pay a fee. That protection is what makes selling possible.

Selling a file does not transfer your copyright. Unless you explicitly sign it away, the buyer gets permission to use the art (whatever your license says), while you keep ownership. That means you can sell the same asset to many customers, or license it differently to different buyers.

A few practical notes: registration (like the US Copyright Office) only matters if you later want to sue for infringement. And if someone pays you to draw something for their game, that is a commission - the rights depend on what your contract says, so put it in writing.

Marketplaces

Where to sell pixel art

itch.io - the most beginner-friendly spot for game assets. Create a store page free, set your own price or "pay what you want," and keep your revenue share on the higher end. Great for sprite packs and tilesets.

Fiverr - the easiest place to sell commissions. Buyers search for "pixel art" and pay per order, so a clear gig with examples and a fast turnaround gets you started quickly.

Etsy - the right home for pixel art as merchandise: prints, stickers, keychains, cross-stitch patterns and decorations. Handmade-style products perform best.

Gumroad - sell anything (packs, tutorials, brushes, fonts) directly with no marketplace search traffic, and keep a large cut. You bring the audience; it handles checkout.

Unity Asset Store / GameDev Market - bigger payouts but stricter requirements. Unity has quality and support standards for listing; GameDev Market is easier to get approved on and aimed squarely at game developers.

Licensing

How licenses work for pixel art

Your license tells buyers what they may and may not do. Pick one per product and state it clearly on the page:

CC0 (public domain) - anyone can use the art commercially or not, with or without credit, and modify it. Maximizes reach; you keep no control.

CC-BY - same freedom, but users must credit you. A good balance for free packs that build your name.

CC-BY-SA - credit plus share-alike: any game that uses the art must also be released under a compatible open license. Rare for commercial games.

Custom standard license - the norm on marketplaces. Typically: royalty-free use in any number of commercial and non-commercial projects, no redistribution of the raw assets, no reselling. You can add tiers like "small team" vs "unlimited / work-for-hire."

Stay Legal

What not to sell

Don't sell ripped sprites. Extracting sprites from games like Pokémon, Mario or Final Fantasy and selling them (even "edited") is copyright and trademark infringement, and marketplaces remove it on DMCA takedowns.

Don't sell fan art of franchises. Fan-made sprites of existing characters are derivative works; selling them commercially is infringement, even if you drew every pixel. Free fan art is fine - selling it usually isn't.

Don't copy asset packs. Re-selling or slightly modifying someone else's pack violates its license. If you used a brush or font, make sure its license allows inclusion in sold assets.

Check AI terms before selling AI art. If your art is AI-assisted, review the generator's commercial-use terms - they vary, and some have changed over time. When in doubt, ask the tool's creator.

Pricing

Can you actually make money at this?

Yes, but think of it as a side income that can grow. New artists typically earn modest amounts from small commissions and $3-$15 asset packs; artists with a recognizable style, a social following and repeat game clients can turn it into a real income.

A few things that move the needle: bundle your assets (a full tileset sells far better than one sprite), show animated GIFs instead of static images, keep a consistent style across packs so buyers come back, and deliver on time on commission sites - that's what earns repeat orders.

Whatever you sell, deliver assets at whole-number integer scales with a clean transparent background. Sellers who hand buyers blurry, anti-aliased or wrongly-sized sprites lose repeat customers fast.

FAQ

Selling questions, answered

Is pixel art copyrighted?

+
Yes. Original pixel art is protected by copyright the moment you create it and fix it in a tangible form - no registration needed. Selling it does not give the buyer ownership of the copyright.

Where can I sell pixel art?

+
Game asset packs sell well on itch.io, Unity Asset Store and GameDev Market. Commissions are easiest through Fiverr. Art prints, stickers and merchandise work on Etsy, and you can sell almost anything directly on Gumroad.

Do I need a license to sell pixel art?

+
If it is your own work, no. You just need a license agreement that tells buyers what they can do with it - for example CC0 (public domain), CC-BY (credit required), or a custom license that limits commercial use or resale.

Can I sell AI-generated pixel art?

+
Usually yes, but check the terms of the specific AI tool you used. Some generators allow commercial use, others restrict it, and a few tools' terms have changed over time. When in doubt, ask the tool's creator.

Can I make money selling pixel art?

+
Yes, though it is usually a side income rather than a full salary. Beginners often earn modest amounts on Fiverr and itch.io; experienced artists with strong portfolios and game clients can make a consistent living.
