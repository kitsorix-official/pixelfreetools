Copyright Policy

Effective Date: August 16, 2026

1. What We Host

PixelFreeTools is a static website. It does not host, store, or display any user-generated content. All image processing is performed 100% client-side in your browser, and images never leave your device. The only content on this Site is the Site's own pages, documentation, and the tool itself.

2. Copyright Ownership

The Site's content, design, and code are the property of PixelFreeTools and its developer. The Site itself does not knowingly display copyrighted material belonging to third parties.

Articles and guides on this Site are original works created by us. Where tools, games, or software are mentioned by name, they belong to their respective owners and are referenced for informational purposes only.

3. Reporting Copyright Infringement (DMCA)

If you believe in good faith that material on this Site infringes your copyright, you may submit a takedown notice. Because the Site hosts no user content, infringement claims are limited to the Site's own pages.

Your notice must be submitted to us and include the following, as required by the DMCA:

  • Identification of the copyrighted work claimed to be infringed.
  • Identification of the material claimed to be infringing and where it is located (URL).
  • Your contact information (name, email address, and mailing address).
  • A statement that you have a good-faith belief that the use is not authorized by the copyright owner, its agent, or the law.
  • A statement, under penalty of perjury, that the information in your notice is accurate and that you are the owner or authorized to act on the owner's behalf.
  • Your physical or electronic signature.

Send your notice through our About page or via our GitHub repository.

4. Our Response

We take copyright notices seriously. Valid notices are reviewed promptly, and infringing material is removed as quickly as possible. Because this is a hobby project run by a single developer, removal may occasionally take a little longer than on commercial platforms - we appreciate your patience.

5. Counter-Notification

If you believe material was removed by mistake or misidentification, you may submit a counter-notification with: identification of the removed material and its previous location, a statement under penalty of perjury that you have a good-faith belief the material was removed by mistake or misidentification, your contact information, and a statement consenting to jurisdiction. Please note that submitting a false counter-notification may expose you to legal liability.

6. Good-Faith Notices

We ask that all copyright complaints be made in good faith. Misrepresentations in takedown notices or counter-notifications can result in legal liability under the DMCA. If you are unsure whether your situation qualifies as infringement, we recommend consulting a lawyer.
