The Picks

The top editors, in detail

Aseprite - the industry standard ($20 once)

The tool most professional pixel artists ship games with. Animation timeline, onion skinning, tilemaps, palettes and Lua scripting. It is open source (you can compile it free), and paid builds cost about $20 as a one-time purchase on Steam or itch.io - no subscription. If you draw sprites regularly, it's worth every dollar.

Pixelorama - the best free editor

Open source (MIT), built with Godot, actively maintained. Layers, onion skinning, animation, rectangular/isometric/hex tilemaps, and non-destructive effects. Runs on Windows, Mac, Linux and in the browser, and exports tilemaps straight to Godot TileSet. The strongest free option in 2026.

LibreSprite - free Aseprite-style workflow

A community fork of Aseprite from when it was still GPL. Same shortcuts and interface, so any Aseprite tutorial applies. Development is slower and it lacks newer Aseprite features (tilemaps, slicing), but for basic drawing and animation it's perfectly capable and free.

Piskel - fastest way to start

A browser editor that works instantly - no account, no install, no ads. Basic (no layers, no pressure sensitivity) but with animation preview and sprite-sheet export. Ideal for game jams and first-time artists.

Pixilart - drawing plus community

Free browser editor and mobile app with a large social community, challenges and sharing. The free tier shows ads; Pro ($4.99/mo) removes them and adds cloud storage.

GraphicsGale - classic Windows editor

A long-running sprite animation editor, now free. Great for frame-by-frame work, though it's Windows-focused and no longer actively developed.

Mobile

Best apps for phone and tablet

Pixel Studio (Android/iOS/desktop) - the most popular mobile editor. Layers, animation, GIF and sprite-sheet export. Free with ads; PRO (a one-time purchase) removes ads and unlocks dark theme, tile mode and bigger canvases.

Pixelc (Android) - free, open source and completely ad-free. Layers, frames, tiling and onion skinning. A great no-cost pick.

Pixilart mobile - the community app; ad-supported on the free tier.

Pixquare (iPad/iPhone) - a paid, native Apple Pencil pixel editor that reads and writes Aseprite files.

Procreate (iPad) - not a pixel editor, but you can make pixel art in it with the right setup (small canvas + 1px hard brush).

You Already Have It

Can I make pixel art in the apps I already use?

Procreate: Yes. Small canvas, turn off StreamLine, 1px hard pencil with no anti-aliasing, zoom in, draw on the grid.

Photoshop: Yes. Small document, hard round 1px brush with anti-alias off, and Image Resample set to "Nearest Neighbor".

Krita: Yes. It even ships dedicated pixel art tools and a grid view - a great free option.

GIMP: Yes. Use the 1px pencil (not the soft brush), disable smoothing, use indexed palettes.

Clip Studio Paint: Yes. Pixel and grid-friendly tools built in.

ibisPaint: Yes, with a 1px pen and the grid; free tier shows ads.

MS Paint: Yes - the classic. Zoom, grid, 1px pencil.

Godot: Yes. The engine includes a pixel art editor and crisp import settings.

Blender and Canva: Possible but not great. Blender is a 3D tool, and Canva's AI "PixelArt Studio" isn't a real editor - use a dedicated tool instead.

AI Tools

What about AI pixel art generators?

General image models (Midjourney, DALL-E, Stable Diffusion) produce pixel-styled images, not true pixel art - edges bleed, pixel sizes vary, and palettes explode. That's fine for concepts, but not for game assets.

Dedicated tools generate on a fixed grid and are more usable: Sprite AI (browser + editor, free tier), Retro Diffusion (pay per image, 16-384px), and PixelLab. Even so, most workflows still finish the last 30% by hand in Aseprite. Always check each tool's terms before selling AI-generated work.

AI upscalers like Real-ESRGAN can enlarge pixel art, but for a pixel-perfect result, nearest-neighbor scaling at whole numbers - exactly what our free tool does - is the right choice.

FAQ

Software questions, answered

What is the best free pixel art software?

+
Pixelorama is the strongest free editor - open source, with layers, animation, tilemaps and a browser version. LibreSprite is a free Aseprite-style fork, and Piskel is the fastest browser option.

Is Aseprite worth the money?

+
For anyone doing sustained sprite animation, yes - it's a one-time ~$20 purchase (no subscription) with the best animation timeline, tilemaps and sprite-sheet export in the business.

Does pixel art cost money?

+
It doesn't have to. Free tools like Piskel, Pixelorama, LibreSprite and GIMP cover everything beginners need. Aseprite costs about $20 once for the paid build.

Does pixel art need wifi?

+
No. Desktop editors work fully offline. Only browser or cloud-based apps (Piskel, Pixilart web) need a connection.

What is the best pixel art app for mobile?

+
Pixel Studio is the most popular (free with ads, paid PRO tier). Pixelc is free, open source and ad-free. Pixilart mobile is community-based and ad-supported.
